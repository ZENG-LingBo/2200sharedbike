The Shared-Bike Collapse
— a systems-thinking case

23 million bikes.
Deployed across 200 Chinese cities by 2017. Within two years, billions of yuan lost, 12 million unrefunded deposits, 300,000 tons of scrap metal, and bike graveyards visible from the air.
Sources: Roland Berger, CKGSB, Reuters · 2017–2019

DIAGRAM 1 · IMPROVEMENTS

Where the architecture should grow

01 · Physical layer

Damage / vibration sensors on every bike

Real-time fleet-health telemetry shifts maintenance from reactive complaint-driven to predictive fault-triage.

02 · Physical-Digital interface

Mandatory docking infrastructure in dense zones

Reduces clutter at source instead of fining it after the fact. Fixes the externality the regulator otherwise has to cap.

03 · Physical layer

Battery-swap stations for the e-bike pivot

Higher per-ride margin, longer dwell window, and a credible path off the unit-economics knife-edge that broke Ofo.

04 · Operational layer

Real-time demand dashboard for operators

Smarter rebalancing decisions, lower truck-mileage cost, fewer over-saturated cluster-zones.

The architecture should embed more guidance into the physical layer — not just the digital one.
DIAGRAM 2 · SOCIAL CLD

The variables in the social system

Variables only. The next five slides walk through which loops bind them — and which archetypes those loops embody.

CLD · LOOP B1

Tragedy of the Commons

Mobike lost 200,000 bikes to theft and vandalism in a single year. The shared resource degraded faster than any single user felt responsible to prevent.

CLD · LOOP R1

Damage Spiral

GoBee shut down its France operation after just four months when half its fleet was destroyed. Visible damage rewires what users believe is normal.

CLD · LOOP R3

Trust Collapse

Ofo took $230M in deposits from 12M users — none recoverable. The "fix" to the cash crunch (stall refunds) caused the panic that ended the company.

CLD · LOOP B2

Regulatory Backlash

Beijing banned new shared-bike deployments in 2017 with 2.3 million bikes already on the streets. The ceiling appeared the moment the externality became politically visible.

CLD · DESIGNED LOOPS

Guidance loops the survivors built

Frontiers in Psychology (2021) confirms what prospect theory predicts: negative feedback (penalty) is more behaviour-shaping than positive (reward). Loss aversion is the lever.

DIAGRAM 2 · IMPROVEMENTS

Loops to break, loops to strengthen

01 · Breaks B1

Credit-based ownership simulation

Treat the credit score as a stake in the system. Sense of ownership becomes synthetic but real — restoring the missing variable.

02 · Breaks R1

Community reporting + faster recovery

Shorten the damage-spiral feedback delay. Broken bikes leave the street before they teach the next user that damage is normal.

03 · Breaks R3

Eliminate deposits entirely (Hellobike's model)

Credit-score-based access. No deposit pool means no deposit pool to collapse. The trust loop has nothing to break.

04 · Strengthens B3

Integrate with Alipay Sesame Score

Make the loss-aversion signal cross-platform. Damage to a bike now threatens loans, rentals, even visa applications — a weight no individual app can carry alone.

Hellobike survived because it engineered the loops Ofo left to chance.
COUPLING · D1 ↔ D2

Where the social and technical systems meet

The shared-bike system didn't fail because of one subsystem. It failed because breakdowns cascaded across the social–technical boundary. System design means designing both sides together.
CONCLUSION

The next generation of shared bikes — e-bikes, credit-based access, geofenced parking — is exactly these system-design improvements in action.

Understanding the system is how we build ones that last.
